# Intro to Data Visualization

## Course Overview

Data visualization is the practice of representing data graphically to make patterns, trends, and outliers easier to understand than they would be in a raw table of numbers. This material covers why visualization matters and how to choose the right chart type for a given question.

## Why Visualization Matters

The human brain processes visual patterns much faster than rows of numbers. A well-designed chart can reveal a trend, an outlier, or a relationship between variables almost instantly — something that might take much longer to notice by scanning a spreadsheet.

Visualization is also a communication tool: a clear chart can convey a finding to someone else far more effectively than a table of raw numbers or a paragraph of text.

## Choosing the Right Chart Type

Different questions call for different chart types:

- **Line chart**: best for showing how a value changes over time (e.g., enrollment numbers by month).
- **Bar chart**: best for comparing values across distinct categories (e.g., number of students per course).
- **Scatter plot**: best for showing the relationship between two numeric variables (e.g., study hours vs. exam score).
- **Pie chart**: best for showing parts of a whole, though bar charts are often a clearer alternative when there are more than a few categories.
- **Histogram**: best for showing the distribution of a single numeric variable (e.g., how exam scores are spread out).

## A Common Mistake

Picking a chart type just because it looks appealing, rather than because it fits the data, often leads to confusing or misleading visuals. For example, using a pie chart with ten thin slices makes it hard to compare values — a bar chart would communicate the same data far more clearly.
